HOW TO PLAY SQUALL

You are the forecaster on duty for Northeast Ohio. Storms move across the radar for two and a half minutes, and your job is to warn each town before severe weather reaches it.

THE GOAL

Finish the shift with public trust above zero and the highest score you can get. You earn points for warning a town before a severe storm arrives. You lose trust when a severe storm hits a town with no warning, and you lose a smaller amount when you warn a town and nothing severe shows up.

READING THE RADAR

Every storm is drawn in the colors a weather radar uses. The strongest color inside a storm tells you how strong it is right now.

Storms come in three shapes. A squall line is a long curved band that can sweep over several towns at once. A supercell is a single large storm with a hook curling off the back, and it always arrives severe. A cluster is a ragged clump that can go either way.

  • The dashed line in front of a storm shows where it is heading.
  • The label above a storm says whether it is growing, fading, or steady. A yellow storm that is growing can turn red before it reaches a town, and a red storm that is fading can drop to yellow.
  • Only the strength at the moment a storm reaches a town counts.

ISSUING WARNINGS

  1. Click a town on the map, or use its button under the map, to put it under a warning.
  2. A warning lasts 14 seconds. The red ring around the town shows how much time is left.
  3. You can have four warnings out at once, so you can't warn every town all the time.
  4. A warning can't be cancelled once it's issued.

The point bonus depends on lead time. A warning issued six or more seconds before the storm arrives earns the full 200 points, and a warning issued at the last second earns 100.

TIPS

  • Watch the west edge of the map, since that is where every storm enters.
  • Follow the dashed line to see which towns a storm will cross before you decide.
  • Warn early on red storms that are growing, and hold off on red storms that are fading and still far away.
  • Storms come faster and stronger late in the shift, so keep at least one warning free.
  • A town is hit as soon as any part of a storm passes over it, so watch the edges of a squall line and not just the middle.
